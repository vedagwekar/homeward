# Homeward — Judge-Focused Answers to Problems 6–10

**Purpose:** Explain a convincing early-stage business thesis, sensible responses to judges' objections, and what funding would help validate.

**Positioning:** These are proposed design choices and hypotheses—not claims of established partnerships, accepted builder terms, validated economics or completed customer research.

## Problem 6: Builders haven't agreed to deposits and retention

### Implementation / answer: Make Homeward valuable to the builder too

A builder will resist a system that only introduces restrictions. Homeward's proposition should include **clear payment expectations, agreed completion criteria and protection against unpaid changes**.

The family and builder agree on material advances, construction stages and payment conditions before work begins. Builders can obtain agreed startup funding, while subsequent payments correspond to clearly defined work. Retention is negotiated around the project rather than imposed as a universal rule.

The stronger idea is that **accountability works both ways**: families deserve evidence of progress, and builders deserve timely decisions and payment for accepted work.

### What to tell judges

> “Builder adoption is central to our model. We intend to offer builders predictable payment milestones, agreed material advances and documented change orders. That gives reliable builders a way to demonstrate their work and reduces disputes over what they were promised. Our first pilots will establish which terms protect families while remaining workable for contractors.”

### What funding would validate

Which payment terms builders accept, whether the workflow reduces payment disagreements, and whether builders subsequently recommend it.

## Problem 7: Custody and cross-border payment partners are unconfirmed

### Implementation / answer: Build the construction-specific service around established financial infrastructure

Homeward's distinctive contribution is connecting **construction evidence, family approval and payment conditions**. The intended business model should assign regulated financial functions to suitable financial institutions.

A phased approach makes this credible: first test inspection coordination and recorded approvals while families pay through their banks; then integrate conditional payments once a suitable partner and legal structure are established.

That makes partnership development a defined research milestone rather than an unexplained missing feature.

### What to tell judges

> “Our intended model is to work with regulated financial partners for custody and settlement, while Homeward manages the construction evidence and approval process. The prototype demonstrates conditional payments; funding would help us validate the legal structure and partner integration for real transactions. We can first test customer demand through a service where families retain control of their bank payments.”

### What funding would validate

Partner suitability, the exact funds flow, integration costs and the legal requirements for an initial corridor.

**Keep the distinction clear:** This is a proposed partnership model, not an existing partnership or compliance approval. The current payment demonstration uses test tokens; it does not establish production INR settlement.

## Problem 8: Disputes create operating costs and legal risk

### Implementation / answer: Make dispute prevention and structured resolution part of the product

Homeward should establish the scope, evidence requirements and approval responsibilities **before disagreements arise**. When a problem occurs, the parties have a shared record of what was agreed, what was inspected and what remains unresolved.

Homeward would coordinate the process. Qualified professionals would assess technical disagreements, and unresolved contractual disputes would follow an agreed external process.

The business hypothesis is that **better records and earlier intervention can make disagreements easier to resolve**. That could reduce the amount of expensive, open-ended mediation the company has to perform.

### What to tell judges

> “We expect disputes, so we are designing for them from the beginning. Clear milestone criteria and documented changes should help prevent avoidable disagreements. When technical findings are challenged, the proposed process uses an independent second review. Homeward would organize the evidence and escalation rather than make every judgment itself. Pilot funding would let us measure those costs and price the service realistically.”

### What funding would validate

Dispute frequency, support hours, independent-review costs and an appropriate allocation of responsibility.

**Important distinction:** Outsourcing judgment does not eliminate Homeward's costs or responsibility. It creates a more credible operating model to investigate. This proposed governance model must also be distinguished from the reviewed prototype's admin settlement authority.

## Problem 9: No established acquisition channel; ₹5,000 CAC is a guess

### Implementation / answer: Reach families through people already involved in their construction decisions

The best initial customer is someone preparing to approve a building quote or send the next substantial payment. Architects, engineers, construction advisers and diaspora networks can potentially introduce Homeward at that moment.

Start with a small paid service—such as checking the evidence behind the next payment—before asking a family to adopt a full-project arrangement.

This also provides a plausible route to repeat business: **a professional can introduce multiple projects even if an individual family builds only once**.

### What to tell judges

> “Our initial distribution hypothesis is professional referrals and diaspora networks. We want to reach families when they are choosing a builder or preparing a major payment, when the need is immediate. A paid first-stage check would let customers experience the value before committing to ongoing coordination. The ₹5,000 acquisition cost is a planning assumption; pilot funding would establish actual conversion rates and acquisition costs.”

### What funding would validate

Which introductions produce paying customers, whether the first service leads to larger engagements, and whether acquisition remains economical beyond friends and family.

## Problem 10: The real competitor is a trusted relative plus WhatsApp

### Implementation / answer: Give the trusted relative better support

The relative already provides trust, local knowledge and access. Homeward should preserve that relationship while helping with the tasks that become difficult: organizing evidence, obtaining technical checks, coordinating several sponsors and explaining payment decisions.

A relative should not have to become a structural engineer, accountant and family mediator simply because they live nearby.

The value proposition becomes **less burden on the local relative and more shared confidence across the family**. WhatsApp can remain the everyday communication channel; Homeward provides the structured record behind important decisions.

### What to tell judges

> “We recognize that most families already rely on a trusted relative and WhatsApp. Homeward is designed to support that relative with independent technical checks, organized records and clear approval responsibilities. The relative keeps their role, but no longer has to carry every decision alone. Our pilot will test whether that support is valuable enough for families to pay for.”

### What funding would validate

Whether families experience less coordination burden, clearer payment decisions and enough additional value to justify the price.

## How to connect these answers in the pitch

**The funding case is that Homeward has identified the key uncertainties and designed focused experiments to resolve them.**

> “The prototype demonstrates the mechanism. Our next step is to test the business around it: builder acceptance, paid customer demand, inspection and dispute costs, and a viable payment-partner structure. Funding would support a focused pilot that turns those assumptions into evidence.”

A credible early-stage position combines **a clear idea, a plausible operating model, and a specific purpose for the funding**.
