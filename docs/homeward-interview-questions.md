# Homeward Real Estate Interview Questions

Prepared November 6, 2026 · Interview owner: Rivan · Status: ready to use; interview not yet conducted

This is a conversation with an experienced Indian real-estate practitioner, not a customer testimonial. His experience can tell us where Homeward fits, where it fails, and whom to speak to next. We have not assumed which states, transaction types, or professional roles he knows firsthand.

## Start here

Allow 40–45 minutes, including the opening and close. Ask the ten numbered questions below; use the optional prompts only when needed. Spend most of the time on one actual project. Avoid sending the pitch or describing the proposed solution until question 8: that could steer the earlier answers. If he has already seen it, record that familiarity and still start with actual past experience.

**Suggested opening:**

> "We're exploring how families abroad manage building or repairing a home in India. We want to understand how it actually works, including what already works well. Your criticism will help more than encouragement. Can we take notes? You can skip anything, and there's no need to name clients or share private documents."

Ask separately before recording. Ask separately before using his name or an exact quote publicly. Default to private notes and an anonymous summary approved by him. Do not put client names, addresses, account details, property papers or unapproved quotes in the public repository.

## The ten core questions

### 1. What kind of real estate work have you personally handled? (3 minutes)

Which cities/states, what role, and how recently? Of the projects you remember from the last two years, roughly how many involved someone funding from abroad?

**Clarify the category:** existing-property sale, developer-built flat/villa purchase, investment/land deal, or construction/renovation on a family's own plot. Which did you personally oversee, and which do you only know about through other people?

If he has no direct self-build or renovation experience, use the nearest example but label it clearly. Ask for an introduction to someone who has that experience; do not turn resale expertise into construction validation.

### 2. Could you walk us through the most recent overseas-funded build or renovation you personally dealt with? (4 minutes)

Start with what the family wanted, the approximate budget, who owned the property, who hired the contractor, and what your involvement was. Where was the payer, and who made decisions locally? Is the project completed, ongoing or abandoned?

**Optional prompts:** Was it fixed-price, labour-only, or a cost-plus contract? Was there a written scope and materials specification? Was it paid from savings, a loan, or both? Did multiple relatives contribute?

### 3. How did one actual payment move, from the request to the final recipient? (5 minutes)

Pick a particular stage. Who asked for money? Who paid whom? What did the payer see before approving it? Who could stop or question it? What happened after payment?

**Optional prompts:** Amount or range; advance versus work completed; bank transfer versus cash; transfer and conversion fees; wait time; invoices, receipts, photos or visits. Did money first go to a relative, the owner's Indian account, the contractor, or a supplier? Did any bank/provider delay or reject it or ask for documents?

We are recording what happened, not asking him to certify that a payment route is legally suitable for Homeward.

### 4. What was the biggest surprise or difficulty on that project, if any? (4 minutes)

How was it discovered? What was the consequence, and what did the family do next? What was still unresolved at the end?

**Only after his description:** Was this a delay, agreed scope change, price increase, quality defect, disputed measurement, title/permission issue, or suspected misuse of money? What evidence distinguished those explanations?

If money was involved, separate the amount paid, amount disputed, value of usable work/materials, amount recovered, and amount still unrecovered as of a stated date. An approximate range or "unknown" is fine. A disputed advance is not automatically a final loss. Ask whether he saw records or is recalling someone else's account.

### 5. Tell us about a similar project that went well. What made the difference? (3 minutes)

What did that family already do to stay informed? Was a trusted relative enough? What would have made an extra inspection or payment service unnecessary?

This is the counterexample: record it even if it weakens the proposal.

### 6. What checks or services do families actually use today, and what do they pay for them? (3 minutes)

Who has the final say on whether work is complete? What have people paid for site supervision, engineering checks, a project manager, bank escrow, or a managed builder? Which did they consider and reject, and why?

**Optional prompts:** Actual quote versus a guess; fee per visit versus percentage; travel and repeat visits; who appointed and paid the checker. What can a remote photo/video establish, and what requires an on-site engineer? For technical judgments outside his expertise, ask whom to consult.

### 7. At what points can work be checked without disrupting the build? (3 minutes)

Using that same project, when would an independent visit have been useful? Who could attend, how much notice would they need, and how long could the contractor reasonably wait?

**Optional prompts:** Checks before concrete covers structural work; absence of an inspector; rain or rescheduling; disagreement over approval; cost of a second visit. Who would resolve a dispute, and who pays while work is paused?

### 8. Here is our idea. Where would it have helped, or made that project worse? (5 minutes)

Read this neutral description:

> "Homeward is a student prototype for families abroad funding work on a home in India. The proposal is to set a budget and payment stages with their chosen builder, pay agreed materials suppliers upfront, and release later stage payments after an independent inspection. The parent or sponsor could request a second check before release. Everyone would see an approval and payment record. The demo uses test money; actual cross-border payments and the production legal arrangements are not in place."

Replay his actual example: which payment or decision would have changed? Which problem would remain? Who would refuse this arrangement, and why? Would the family trust a new service to hold or control money?

**Choose up to two stress tests:**

- Would direct supplier payment fit the contractor's pricing and credit arrangements? Who handles delivery, wrong materials, theft, returns and refunds?
- If a builder quits or a supplier's bank details change, what must happen? Our demo fixes payees; would that make a real project unworkable?
- Would a parent feel comfortable withholding approval? What if they are unavailable, pressured, or disagree with the engineer?
- Whose approval should be enough to release money, whose objection should pause it, and who decides if they disagree?
- What if a sponsor stops funding, the rupee budget changes, or a second engineer disagrees? What should happen to money already spent versus still held?

### 9. Who would actually buy this, and what would they choose instead? (4 minutes)

Ask his unprompted view of an appropriate price first. Then explain the proposed price accurately:

> "Our tentative platform fee is 1.5% of money released: ₹22,500 if the full ₹15 lakh budget is released. Inspections, foreign exchange and payment-partner costs would be additional and are not yet quoted."

Does that change his answer? For the family in the actual case, who decides and which alternative would they likely choose: Homeward, inspection-only, an existing supervisor, a bank/managed-builder option, or the current arrangement? What has he observed that supports that prediction? What would need to be proven first? If he personally funded a relevant project, record his own buyer response separately.

Treat his answer as expert opinion, not evidence that NRI senders will pay. We cannot test acceptance of the all-in price until inspection and payment-provider quotes exist. Do not ask him to spend money or commit anyone to a real-money pilot.

### 10. What are we missing, and whom should we speak to next? (3 minutes)

What is the strongest reason this could fail? Which type of family and location should we start with, or avoid?

Would he be comfortable making introductions to one actual overseas sender, one local builder, and one independent engineer? A loan officer or payments professional would also help. Let him ask them first; do not collect their private details without consent.

Close by reading back the two or three main things you heard. Ask what you misunderstood and whether an anonymous summary can be used in the challenge. Confirm any proposed quote separately in its final wording.

## If he only has 15 minutes

Use these compressed prompts rather than trying to fit the full questions into less time:

| Time | Ask |
|---|---|
| 1 minute | Permission for notes, his role/region and relevant direct experience |
| 5 minutes | One recent project: who paid whom, for what, and on what proof? |
| 3 minutes | What went wrong, what worked, and what was the actual outcome? |
| 4 minutes | Read the neutral concept in question 8; what would it improve or make worse in that case? |
| 2 minutes | Main objection, consent to an anonymous summary, and one useful introduction |

Skip pricing rather than squeeze out the actual project story.

## Optional follow-ups for another conversation

Use only those relevant to his experience. These are research prompts, not extra mandatory questions.

| Topic | Question worth asking | What it would change |
|---|---|---|
| Scope and ownership | Who signs the construction agreement and approves variations when the sponsor is not the owner? | Project setup and decision rights |
| Stage definitions | Can you show a redacted example of a stage schedule or inspection checklist, with permission? | Whether our sample stages match practice |
| Supplier economics | Does the builder rely on supplier credit, bulk discounts or materials margins? | Whether supplier-first payment helps or removes their working capital |
| Practical payments | Are wages paid individually, by a foreman or by the contractor? Are invoices available? | Whether proposed split payments match actual obligations |
| Defects | How long after completion do serious defects emerge, and what retention is actually accepted? | Whether 10% held for 30 days is useful or arbitrary |
| Proof and fraud | Could a contractor stage a convincing video while other work is poor? What would an inspector need beyond the video? | Limits of random codes and remote evidence |
| Trust | Who checks the checker, and what conflicts would disqualify an inspector? | Assignment and second-check rules |
| Family relationships | Does formal oversight reduce pressure on relatives, or feel like an accusation? | Product language and adoption |
| Existing protection | In this particular transaction, what bank, contractual or RERA processes already applied? | Avoid duplicating protections or mixing transaction types; verify legal interpretation separately |
| Currency | Who absorbs changes in exchange rates or local prices over a long build? | Budget and change-order design |

## Turn the conversation into evidence

Within a day, create a short anonymous case summary for his review. Separate his observations from our interpretation. Give each additional project its own case ID but count this as one interview, not multiple independent respondents. Deduplicate stories later repeated by another interviewee.

Only add an approved summary to [the evidence ledger](homeward-evidence.md). A useful entry reads:

> "One family-network practitioner, with direct experience in [region/type], described [specific process]. This suggests [hypothesis]; it does not establish national prevalence."

Do not publish that sentence until the blanks have real answers and consent.

Next validation should include actual paying senders, builders and engineers. Ask the same core questions across participants and keep refusals and negative answers. Krish's dad can be a valuable guide without representing every NRI family or acting as an engineer, lender or legal adviser.
