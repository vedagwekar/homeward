homeward
Pay for your parents' house one checked stage at a time.
How one stage works
1The family locks the money in a Stellar contract.
2The stage opens. The supplier is paid for materials.
3The builder films the one-time stage code on site.
4An independent engineer checks the work and decides.
5The parent (Amma) says Yes or taps Check again. A second engineer must then agree.
6One payment pays builder and workers, with a certificate. Or the money stays locked.

Amma decides

Two big buttons, her language. "Nothing is paid until you say yes."

The engineer looks, ticks, decides

Paid the same fee whether they pass or fail the work.

The family sees every rupee

Paid, in progress, locked, not yet funded. Held by the contract.

What the contract enforces
✓No stage payment without the engineer and the parent.
✓Payees are fixed when the build is set up. There is no withdraw function, not even for us.
✓Nobody who pays, builds or supplies can be the engineer. A second check needs a different person.
✓If the builder walks off, their deposit and the unspent money go back to the family.

What we have learned

16 conversations: 15 engineers (Mumbai 6, Delhi 5, Kerala 2, Canada 2) and a father who built from Canada.

"Easy to send money, very, very hard to get money back."

Krish's father, quoted with permission

Engineers said it would save them hours of chasing approvals. None quoted a price, and we haven't tested the fee on families yet.

Break-even

About 100 builds a year on ₹30 lakh builds with a small team: roughly 1.4% of our Kerala estimate. On ₹15 lakh builds with the fee alone we would need about 27%, so we don't pitch that. Every input is our assumption.
