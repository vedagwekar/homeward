# Homeward: the business case

Written 15 Nov 2026 for the finals on 18 Nov. Covers who we serve, how big the market is, what we can charge, how many builds we need to break even, and how each part of the judging rubric is answered.

**How to read the numbers.** Every figure is tagged:
- **[V]** verified in a source (linked at the end)
- **[C]** calculated from verified figures (the sum is shown)
- **[A]** my assumption, not data. Each one says how to test it.

Your evidence ledger warns against claiming "a baseless TAM". This case follows that: it starts from verified money flows, narrows with labelled assumptions, and shows a low, base and high range. It does not claim a precise market.

## 1. The answer in six lines

1. **Niche:** Kerala-origin family members in Canada, the Gulf and the US who are building or extending a parent's house on the family's own plot, with their own builder, for roughly ₹10 to 40 lakh.
2. **Money already flowing:** ₹2,16,893 crore reached Kerala in 2023 [V], and renovating houses and shops was the largest listed use at 15.8% [V]. That is about **₹34,269 crore a year** [C] into the exact thing we protect.
3. **Qualified builds a year in Kerala:** about **6,850** in the base case, with a range of **1,700 to 20,600** [C from assumptions]. This is the number to test in the pilot.
4. **Break-even:** with a small team, builds averaging ₹30 lakh, and a margin on engineer visits, about **100 to 300 builds a year**, which is **1.4% to 4.3%** of the base Kerala market [C].
5. **The honest warning:** on ₹15 lakh builds, with fee income only and a normal team, we would need **27%** of the base market. The 1.5% fee alone does not carry a company. Bigger builds, an engineer-visit margin, and more states are what make it work.
6. **Price check:** the family's all-in cost is **3.5% to 5.5%** of the budget [C]. That is at or above the top of the 1% to 4% that consultants quote for project supervision [V, vendor blogs], and below the 4.4% to 6.5% families already pay to move money [V].

## 4. Pricing: what families pay now, and what we charge

| What families already pay | Price | Source |
|---|---|---|
| One home inspection | ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 | customer reports, evidence R01 and R02 |
| NRI villa check package | ₹14,999 | advertised, R07 |
| NRI remote site oversight | ₹8,000 to ₹20,000 a month | advertised, R06 |
| Project supervision consultancy | 1% to 4% of project cost | vendor blogs [V, weak] |
| Moving money from abroad to India | average 6.36% to 6.49% globally for US$200; a comparison site cites a 3.87% median for US to India | World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide |

**What Homeward charges:** 1.5% of money locked, charged as it is funded, plus engineer visits at a fixed price. On a ₹15 lakh build with six ₹10,000 visits that is ₹22,500 + ₹60,000 = **5.5%** of the budget. On ₹30 lakh it is **3.5%** [C].

**What this tells us:**
- Engineer visits, not our fee, are most of what the family pays. We must show they get something a WhatsApp group and a bank transfer cannot give.
- Because our all-in price is at the top of the consultant range, small builds are a poor fit. Aim at **₹25 to 40 lakh** builds. A 1,500 sq ft Kerala house at ₹1,800 to ₹2,500 per sq ft is about **₹27 to ₹37.5 lakh** [C from sources].
- A minimum fee fixes small builds. Suggested for the pilot: **the greater of 1.5% or ₹20,000** (a pilot billing rule; the contract's fee is a percentage today).

**Prices to test in the pilot** (not validated offers):

| Option | What the family pays | Why test it |
|---|---|---|
| A. As built | 1.5% + visits | simplest to explain |
| B. Flat case fee | ₹25,000 per build + visits | predictable, protects small builds |
| C. Care plan | ₹8,000 a month during the build + visits | matches what competitors already sell |

## 6. Break-even

Yearly fixed costs [A]: **Lean ₹30 lakh** (one developer, one coordinator, legal advice, tools), **Base ₹90 lakh** (two developers, an ops lead and two coordinators, compliance, insurance, marketing), **Funded ₹2.5 crore** (adds partner integration and a second corridor).

**Builds needed a year, and the share of the base Kerala market (6,850 builds):**

| Team | ₹15 lakh, fee only | ₹15 lakh, with visit margin | ₹30 lakh, fee only | ₹30 lakh, with visit margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lean, ₹30 lakh | 612 (8.9%) | 216 (3.1%) | 140 (2.0%) | **99 (1.4%)** |
| Base, ₹90 lakh | 1,837 (26.8%) | 647 (9.4%) | 421 (6.1%) | **296 (4.3%)** |
| Funded, ₹2.5 crore | 5,102 (74%) | 1,799 (26%) | 1,168 (17%) | 822 (12%) |

**In money terms:** the Base team at ₹30 lakh with the margin needs about ₹89 crore through the platform a year, which is **0.26% of the ₹34,269 crore** Kerala renovation flow [C].

**Against the range.** At 296 builds, the Base team needs 17% of the low market and 1.4% of the high market. The market estimate matters as much as the cost estimate, which is why the pilot funnel is the first priority.

**Take rate needed at a given share of the base market** (Base team, ₹15 lakh builds): 1% share needs about 9.8% of budget; 2% needs 5.4%; 5% needs 2.8%; 10% needs 1.9%. We charge 1.5% plus margin. So at ₹15 lakh we need about 5% of the market or more.

**What moves break-even most, in order:**
1. **Average build size.** ₹30 lakh needs a third of the builds that ₹15 lakh does.
2. **The engineer-visit margin.** About 9,000 per build, worth more than our fee on small builds.
3. **Team size.** Lean costs a third of Base.
4. **More states.** Multiplies the market (section 3) if behaviour is similar.

## 7. The cost of being a real product

- **Holding customers' rupees needs a licence or a partner.** RBI's September 2025 payment aggregator directions require ₹15 crore net worth at application, rising to ₹25 crore by year three, for a payment aggregator including cross-border [V]. That is out of reach for us, so the plan is a **licensed partner**, not our own licence. The partner's cut is in the unit economics above as 0.25%.
- **The builder's deposit also needs to be held by a licensed party** in real rupees.
- **Materials are most of the spend.** MoSPI says about 75% of own-account household construction spending is materials [V, broad definition], while our demo's materials are 31% of the budget [C]. In real builds more money goes to suppliers when a stage opens. That raises early risk and means the 5% deposit may be too low. Pair it with purchase-order approval and delivery checks, as your evidence file recommends.
- **Kerala's building rules** call for a registered site-supervising professional [V]. Homeward's audits are an extra owner-side service and do not replace that.

## 9. Likely business questions, and honest answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How big is the market? | About ₹34,269 crore a year flows into home renovation in Kerala alone. We estimate 1,700 to 20,600 qualified builds a year, and our pilot measures the real rate. |
| What share do you need to break even? | About 1% to 4% of Kerala with a lean team and ₹30 lakh builds. We would not make it on ₹15 lakh builds with the fee alone. |
| Why would a family pay 3.5% to 5.5%? | They already pay about 4% to 6% to move the money, and consultants charge 1% to 4% for supervision. We add the part neither does: the payment cannot leave without the engineer and the parent. |
| Why would a builder accept a deposit? | Payment is guaranteed and locked before work starts, disputes follow rules fixed in advance, and the deposit comes back in full. We haven't asked builders yet. |
| Can you hold the money? | Not yet. It needs a licensed partner under RBI rules. |
| Why Stellar? | A public, verifiable rule that pays several people in one transaction at near-zero cost, with anchors as a route to local payout. |
| What if a builder disappears? | The deposit and unspent money come back to the family. That reduces the loss. It does not guarantee full recovery. |
| What do you know least? | How many families fit, and what a builder will accept. Both are what the pilot measures. |
