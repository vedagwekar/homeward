# Homeward: five prototype sessions

Written 13 Nov 2026. Goal: five 15-minute sessions before finals on 18 Nov, so the pitch can report real reactions to the prototype instead of one interview.

## Who to ask

People who have paid for, or are about to pay for, a build or repair for family in another country. In order of usefulness:

1. Krish's father (second session, this time with the prototype)
2. Anyone who funded a build or renovation abroad in the last three years
3. Anyone planning one in the next year
4. A parent or relative who was the person on site

Not useful: friends who have never sent money for a build. Their opinion is about the screens, not the problem.

## Before you start

- Open `docs/design-drafts/07-3d-and-photos.html` on a phone or laptop.
- Say: "We're students testing an idea. It isn't a real product and nothing here moves real money. Criticism helps more than praise."
- Ask: "Can we take notes? Can we quote you, with or without your name?" Write down the answer.
- One person talks, one person takes notes. Do not explain the app until question 4.

## The session (15 minutes)

**Their last project (5 minutes). Ask about what happened, not what they think.**

1. Tell me about the last time you paid for building or repair work from abroad. Who was on site for you?
2. How did you decide when to send each payment? What did you see before you sent it?
3. Did you pay anyone to check the work, such as an engineer, a supervisor or a relative's expenses? Roughly how much?

**The prototype (6 minutes). Hand them the device and stay quiet.**

4. "This is a house being paid for in stages. Tell me what you think is going on." Note what they understand without help, and where they stop.
5. Ask them to add money for the roof slab. Note whether they read the fee line, and what they say about it.
6. Switch to the parent's phone at the roof step. "Would your parent use this? What would they do here?"
7. Show the roof being rejected. "What do you think happened to the money?"

**Price and commitment (4 minutes).**

8. "On a ₹15 lakh build the fee is ₹22,500, and each engineer visit is quoted separately, about ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 from what we've seen advertised. Is that something you would have paid on your last project? Why or why not?"
9. "If we ran a pilot with ten families, would you put down a refundable deposit to hold a place?" A yes here is worth far more than "I like it".
10. "Who else should we talk to?"

## How to report it

Report counts and reasons, including the noes. For example: "Five sessions. Four understood the stages without help. Three would have paid the fee on their last project. Two said no: one trusts his brother, one thought the engineer visits cost too much. One would put down a deposit."

Then name one thing the team changed because of what people said. The rubric asks for evidence that the team learned and iterated.

Do not say pricing is validated, and do not turn "I like it" into demand. Five sessions is a small, friendly sample. Say so.

## Pass marks we set earlier

From `assumptions.md`: at least 3 of 5 relevant people say they would seriously consider the fee once they have seen a full example. If fewer do, that is a finding to report, not a failure to hide.
