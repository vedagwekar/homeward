# HOMEWARD

## Sending money home is easy. Knowing what it builds is harder.

**Homeward links overseas-funded home construction to documented milestones, independent inspections, and family payment approvals — without requiring families to replace their chosen builder.**

### The problem | A receipt is not proof of progress

Families abroad can confirm transfers instantly, yet assess construction through scattered photos, phone calls, and contractor assurances. In a **2026 Kerala consumer case**, a US-resident homeowner's local brother reported paying **₹34.52 lakh** toward a **₹37.25-lakh** contract. Work remained unfinished and leaking; independent expert evidence was central to the ruling. This is a documented example, **not** a national fraud-rate estimate. [1]

### The solution | Agree → Fund → Verify → Release

1. **Agree:** Sponsors, parents, and builders set scope, payees, budgets, and milestones. Payees are fixed once the build is set up.
2. **Fund:** Family members contribute; the supplier receives the agreed materials advance when a stage starts.
3. **Verify:** Builders submit coded video evidence; an independent engineer checks the site. A parent can request a second check by a different engineer.
4. **Release or hold:** A Stellar smart contract releases each stage's labour payment only after the required approvals, paying builder and labour together and recording a certificate. Rejected or disputed stages stay locked.

**The value:** More visibility for overseas sponsors, meaningful control for parents, and clearer payment expectations for builders.

### Opportunity | A focused entry market

**Initial customer:** Diaspora families in Canada financing self-managed home builds or renovations in Kerala. India received **US$135.4B in remittances in FY2024–25** (all purposes, **not** Homeward's addressable market). Kerala's 2023 migration survey identifies **renovation of houses/shops as 15.8%** of its reported remittance-use distribution (not only new construction). [2][3]

Banks already use construction-stage verification, and independent NRI monitoring services exist. Homeward's proposed distinction is the **combination** of chosen-builder flexibility, shared family funding, independent checks, parent re-checks, and programmable stage payments. [4]

### Business model | Designed to be affordable, still to be validated

A **proposed 1.5% Homeward fee** on money locked covers the locked account, checks, and records. A hypothetical **₹15-lakh funded project = ₹22,500 gross fees**, before payout, FX, tax, and operating costs. **Engineer visits are quoted separately** at a fixed price agreed up front, paid through Homeward and never through the builder; the engineer is paid the same whether they approve or reject. Pricing and willingness to pay are **not yet validated**. Potential distribution: diaspora networks and local inspection collaborators; **no partnerships are claimed**.

### Prototype and proof | Working on Stellar Testnet

The web app runs on a **Soroban contract on Stellar Testnet**, backed by **33 automated contract tests**. From the app, every action is a real testnet transaction with a Stellar Expert link: two family members fund a build, the supplier is paid when a stage starts, and one engineer-plus-parent approval pays **builder and labour in a single transaction** with an on-chain certificate. When a parent taps **"check again"** and the second engineer rejects, the money **stays locked**. Stopping a build returns unspent money pro-rata (contract-tested). **Interac, INR payouts, SMS, engineer licences, and images are simulated.** Production custody and payout compliance require a licensed partner, not yet confirmed.

**Next validation:** Test with overseas families, builders, independent inspectors, and a qualified payout provider; measure willingness to pay, wrongful-release prevention, cost, and dispute handling.

> **A home is built in stages. Confidence should be, too.**
