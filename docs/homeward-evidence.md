# Homeward Proposal Evidence

Prepared and checked November 6, 2026 · Owner: Rivan · Status: desk research; customer validation pending

This document is the claim-by-claim evidence record for the Homeward proposal: the source, what it supports, what it does not support, and what we still need to learn.

## What the evidence currently says

There is credible evidence of a large remittance market, renovation spending in Kerala, and established lender practices linking construction payments to verified progress. That makes Homeward a plausible problem to investigate. It does not yet prove the frequency of losses in our target segment, demand for Homeward, willingness to pay, or a viable production payment route.

**No reliable national estimate of losses specifically from NRI-funded family construction or renovation was established in this review.** Do not invent one, use total cybercrime/banking-fraud losses as a substitute, or add newspaper allegations into a national total.

The supplied briefs describe the planned build. No working contract, test results, customer interviews, partner agreements or live transactions were supplied or verified for this deliverable. "Planned on testnet" must not become "already works."

## Evidence labels

- **Verified source:** the source text was inspected and supports the narrowly worded claim; this is not independent auditing of the underlying data.
- **Company claim:** a provider describes its own offering; performance and availability still require checking.
- **Reported allegation:** a news report describes a complaint, not an adjudicated loss.
- **Calculation:** our arithmetic, with stated inputs; not a separately measured statistic.
- **Hypothesis / pending:** needs interviews, quotes, technical proof or qualified review.

## 2. Fraud and loss: what can honestly be said

### E09: the ₹75 lakh example in the existing pitch needs correction

[The Tribune, July 11, 2021](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/chandigarh/nri-accuses-sister-of-duping-him-of-rs75l-lodges-plaint-281250/) reports that a Canada-based man alleged relatives failed to reimburse him for beautification work he arranged and paid for on **their** under-construction house while they were abroad. The report describes an FIR; we did not establish the eventual outcome.

This is a construction-related family reimbursement dispute. It does **not** substantiate the pitch's story of relatives keeping money he sent from abroad to build his parents' home. Remove that interpretation from the hook. If retained at all, use it as a clearly labelled adjacent example, not direct evidence of Homeward's exact target failure.

### E10: why broad crime totals are not the missing number

[RBI banking publications](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23648), Appendix Table IV.7, note 4, explicitly distinguish reported fraud amounts from actual losses. This is not an estimate of Homeward-addressable losses. Broad crime totals also cannot be used without checking whether they isolate overseas-funded self-build projects; "property lost" does not necessarily mean real estate.

**Source-access limitation:** MHA/NCRB economic-offence material appeared in search-index results, but the MHA report links tested returned 404 on direct opening. No numerical claim from those reports is treated as verified or used here.

The targeted search covered the existing case, RBI remittance/banking publications, MHA/NCRB-related results and NRI construction/real-estate loss queries. It did not establish a suitable national total. This is a bounded research result, not proof that no such dataset exists.

For interviews, record **paid, disputed, recovered, usable work/materials, and still unrecovered** separately. Keep delay, overrun, defective work, missing funds, title disputes and developer insolvency distinct. Do not estimate losses prevented by Homeward merely because a bad outcome occurred before Homeward existed.

## 5. Pricing and market calculations

**Illustrative economics, not validated willingness to pay:** ₹15,00,000 × 1.5% = ₹22,500 platform revenue if the entire build budget is released and charged once. A hypothetical 100 such fully released builds gives ₹22,50,000 gross platform fees, before operating costs. Neither is a profit estimate or a forecast.

Do not double-charge materials once upfront and again as part of stage release. Clarify the fee treatment of cancellations/refunds and whether fees are charged on top or deducted. The briefs differ in emphasis between fee on funding and fee per release; resolve before publishing payment examples.

The earlier 1–3% inspection range came from a generic guide cited in the supplied brief, not local quotes; that guide was not independently verified here. Keep the range out of an asserted all-in price. If modelled, label it a scenario: 1.5% platform + 1–3% inspections = 2.5–4.5% **before** FX, provider fees and any applicable taxes. Show rupee amounts until a dated exchange rate is checked. Acceptance of an all-in price cannot be tested until quotes exist.

Do not multiply India-wide remittances × Canada share × Kerala share × renovation share into a construction market size: the geography, populations and definitions do not establish that intersection. A defensible early annual revenue model should start with:

> reachable qualified families × fraction starting a relevant project that year × mean funded project budget × adoption rate × fee rate

Those inputs are currently missing; project counts and fractions must not be multiplied interchangeably.

## 6. What counts as technical evidence

The proposed real/simulated split below is copied as a **target**, not a build-status claim.

| Capability | Proof required before claiming it works |
|---|---|
| Testnet funding by two sponsors and supplier payment | Network + deployed contract ID + transaction links + balances showing actual transfers |
| Approval-triggered split payment | Successful transaction and recipient balances; failing unauthorized/insufficient-approval tests |
| Parent re-check and second inspector | Demo and tests showing release blocked until the correct additional approval; rejection stays locked |
| Freeze/refund and handover hold | Boundary tests, refund arithmetic, and transactions if presented as working |
| Stage certificate | Queryable record tied to report hash and transaction; avoid implying the chain verified construction |
| SEP-10/SEP-24 test-anchor deposit | Auth/deposit flow and completed test deposit evidence; distinguish anchor test assets from Homeward's test token |

Interac, INR payouts, SMS, KYC/bank verification, inspector credentials/selection and production custody are not verified capabilities here. Keep simulations labelled. A testnet transfer to a payout account does not prove an Indian builder received rupees. Backend-held demo keys do not prove production-grade independent control.
